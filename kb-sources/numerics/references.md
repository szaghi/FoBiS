# References

The publications cited by the documents of this pack, by topic.

## Rounding error and floating-point arithmetic

- Goldberg, D. (1991). What every computer scientist should know about floating-point arithmetic. ACM Computing
  Surveys 23(1), 5-48.
- Higham, N. J. (2002). Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd edition. SIAM. Chapter 4: summation.
- Kahan, W. (1965). Further remarks on reducing truncation errors. Communications of the ACM 8(1), 40.
- Demmel, J., Nguyen, H. D. (2015). Parallel reproducible summation. IEEE Transactions on Computers 64(7),
  2060-2070.

## Stochastic arithmetic and significant digits

- Parker, D. S., Pierce, B., Eggert, P. R. (2000). Monte Carlo arithmetic: how to gamble with floating point and
  win. Computing in Science and Engineering 2(4), 58-68.
- Denis, C., de Oliveira Castro, P., Petit, E. (2016). Verificarlo: checking floating point accuracy through
  Monte Carlo arithmetic. Proceedings of the 23rd IEEE Symposium on Computer Arithmetic (ARITH 23), 55-62.
- Jézéquel, F., Chesneaux, J.-M. (2008). CADNA: a library for estimating round-off error propagation. Computer
  Physics Communications 178(12), 933-955.
- Sohier, D., de Oliveira Castro, P., Févotte, F., Lathuilière, B., Petit, E., Jamond, O. (2021). Confidence
  intervals for stochastic arithmetic. ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 47(2), article 10.
- Wilson, E. B., Hilferty, M. M. (1931). The distribution of chi-square. Proceedings of the National Academy of
  Sciences 17(12), 684-688.
